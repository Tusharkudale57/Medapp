Cardiac Life Support Protocol Pediatric Life Support Protocol ICU Care & Ventilation Protocols High-Risk Drugs Dosing Protocols Obstetric Emergencies & PPH Protocol Acute Ischemic Stroke & tPA Guidelines Advanced Trauma Life Support Guidelines

1. VF/pVT (Vibrillating/Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia)
  • Shock → CPR 2 min (IV/IO access) → Shock → CPR 2 min (Epinephrine 1mg Q3-5m) → Shock → CPR 2 min (Amiodarone 300mg or Lidocaine 1-1.5mg/kg).
2. PEA / Asystole
  • CPR 2 min → Epinephrine 1mg Q3-5m as soon as possible → Rhythm check (Shockable? If yes, go to VF/pVT, otherwise CPR 2m).
3. Post-Cardiac Arrest Care
  • Optimize ventilation (SpO2 92-98%, PaCO2 35-45 mmHg) → Maintain coronary perfusion (MAP ≥ 65 mmHg) → Targeted Temperature Management (32°C - 36°C for 24h).
1. Neonatal Resuscitation Program (NRP) Guidelines
  • Warm, dry, stimulate → Assess breathing/HR (HR < 100 or apnea: Positive Pressure Ventilation for 30s) → HR < 60: Chest compressions (3:1 ratio, 90 compressions + 30 breaths/min).
2. Pediatric Septic Shock Protocol
  • First hour: 20mL/kg crystalloid boluses up to 60mL/kg → Start Epinephrine (cold shock) or Norepinephrine (warm shock) infusion → Add Stress dose Hydrocortisone (if fluid/vasopressor refractory).
3. Pediatric Airway Calculations
  • Uncuffed ETT Size: (Age / 4) + 4 mm • Cuffed ETT Size: (Age / 4) + 3.5 mm.
1. Ventilator Settings in ARDS (Lung Protective Ventilation)
  • Low Tidal Volume: 4 - 6 mL/kg Predicted Body Weight • Plateau Pressure limit ≤ 30 cmH2O • High PEEP titration strategy • Permissive Hypercapnia (maintain pH ≥ 7.20).
2. Ventilator Weaning Criteria
  • Cause of respiratory failure reversed • Intact airway protective reflexes • RSBI (Rapid Shallow Breathing Index) < 105 • Successful 30-120 min Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT) on T-piece or PSV.
3. Surviving Sepsis Hour-1 Bundle
  • Measure lactate → Obtain blood cultures prior to antibiotics → Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics → Fluid resuscitation (30mL/kg crystalloid for MAP < 65 or lactate ≥ 4).
1. Vasoactive Drug Infusions
  • Norepinephrine: 0.05 - 0.5 mcg/kg/min (first line for septic shock).
  • Dopamine: 2 - 20 mcg/kg/min (renal, inotropic, and pressor ranges).
  • Dobutamine: 2 - 20 mcg/kg/min (inodilator for cardiogenic shock).
2. Crucial CYP450 Drug Interactions
  • CYP3A4 Inhibitors: Ketoconazole, Clarithromycin, Amiodarone (elevates levels of statins, calcium channel blockers).
  • CYP3A4 Inducers: Rifampin, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine (reduces efficacy of oral anticoagulants, oral contraceptives).
1. Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) Management
  • Uterine massage, early cord clamping, and Oxytocin (10 U IM or IV infusion).
  • Refractory bleeding: Tranexamic Acid (TXA) 1g IV over 10 min, Methylergonovine 0.2mg IM, Carboprost 250 mcg IM, and uterine balloon tamponade.
2. Hypertensive Crisis & Eclampsia
  • For severe hypertension (BP ≥ 160/110 mmHg): Labetalol 20mg IV over 2 min OR Hydralazine 5-10mg IV.
  • Seizure prophylaxis: Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4) 4-6g IV bolus over 20 min, then 1-2g/h continuous infusion.
3. Shoulder Dystocia (ALERTM Steps)
  • Help → Leg flexion (McRoberts maneuver) → Suprapubic pressure → Enter (internal rotation maneuvers) → Remove posterior arm → Roll patient onto all fours.
1. Stroke Activation & Diagnostics
  • Rapid FAST screening & stroke team activation → Obtain non-contrast Head CT within 20 mins of arrival to rule out intracranial hemorrhage.
2. Thrombolytic Therapy (tPA / Tenecteplase)
  • Administer IV Alteplase (0.9 mg/kg, max 90mg; 10% bolus, 90% over 60 mins) within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.
  • Strict Blood Pressure Management: Keep BP < 185/110 mmHg before thrombolysis and < 180/105 mmHg for the first 24 hours.
3. Endovascular Thrombectomy
  • Large Vessel Occlusion (LVO) in anterior circulation → Perform mechanical thrombectomy within 24 hours based on CT perfusion mismatch.
1. Primary Trauma Survey (ABCDE)
  • Airway: C-spine stabilization, jaw thrust/intubation.
  • Breathing: Treat tension pneumothorax, open chest wounds, flail chest.
  • Circulation: Hemorrhage control, 2 large-bore IVs, warm crystalloids, and early blood transfusion.
  • Disability: Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) and pupillary response.
  • Exposure: Undress patient fully to inspect, but prevent hypothermia.
2. Hemorrhagic Shock Classification
  • Class I (<15% blood loss, normal vitals) • Class II (15-30% loss, tachycardia, normal BP) • Class III (30-40% loss, hypotension, confusion) • Class IV (>40% loss, severe shock, anuria).
3. Needle Decompression
  • Immediate treatment of tension pneumothorax: insert 14G/16G needle into the 5th intercostal space at the anterior axillary line.
